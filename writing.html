How to write an essay

Title: Write the chosen topic clearly. Paragraph 1: Introduce it in two or three sentences. Paragraphs 2–3: Give distinct points with a simple example. Last paragraph: Conclude without introducing a new idea. Use the word limit printed on your paper; no fixed half-yearly length is confirmed.

How to answer a conversation passage

Read the speakers and setting, then find each answer in the dialogue. Number your answers and write complete sentences. For a word-meaning question, copy the word exactly from the passage.

Part II · Writing and practice

Four original model essays (approximately 160 words each)

1. Women Empowerment (a 2025 board choice)

Women empowerment means giving women the freedom and opportunity to shape their own lives. It begins at home, where a daughter deserves the same encouragement to learn and dream as a son.

Education opens doors, but opportunity must follow it. A young woman should be able to choose a profession, earn fairly and make decisions about her future. Safe public spaces, respectful workplaces and access to healthcare are equally important. Families can help by sharing household work instead of treating it as a woman's duty alone.

Empowerment is not a contest between women and men. When a woman can use her abilities, her family and community benefit too. A teacher guides children, an entrepreneur creates jobs, and a leader can speak for those whose voices are seldom heard.

Laws matter, but attitudes matter just as much. We should challenge unfair jokes, listen to girls and support their ambitions. A society grows stronger when everyone has room to grow.

2. Global Warming and the Future of Mankind (a 2025 board choice)

Global warming is the gradual rise in Earth's temperature. Human activities, especially burning coal, oil and gas, add heat-trapping gases to the atmosphere. The result is not only hotter days; changing weather can affect farms, homes and health.

In Assam, a flood can interrupt classes and damage a family's livelihood. Elsewhere, drought may leave farmers without enough water. Wildlife also suffers when forests and habitats change. Poor families often have the fewest resources to recover from such losses.

There is no single magic solution. Governments must support cleaner energy, protect forests and prepare communities for extreme weather. We can save electricity, avoid waste and choose public transport when practical. Planting trees helps, but protecting mature trees matters as well.

The future of mankind depends on choices made today. We do not need to live without comfort; we need to use resources wisely. If people work together, a safer and fairer future is still possible.

3. Youth Empowerment (a 2026 board choice)

Young people are often called the future of a country, but they are also part of its present. Youth empowerment means giving them knowledge, skills and a genuine chance to take part in decisions.

Education should teach more than answers for an examination. Students need to ask questions, work with others and learn practical skills. A village youth group, for example, can organise a reading corner or help keep a riverbank clean. Such work builds confidence and a sense of responsibility.

Adults have a role too. They should listen without dismissing every new idea, provide safe spaces for discussion and guide young people through mistakes. Access to training and fair employment matters more than empty praise. Young women and men must receive equal opportunities.

Young people also need to use freedom responsibly. They should check information before sharing it and respect different opinions. With support and self-discipline, their energy can become useful action that improves both their lives and their communities.

4. India's North-East as a Tourism Destination (a 2026 board choice)

India's North-East invites visitors with its rivers, hills, forests and living traditions. In Assam, the Brahmaputra shapes daily life, while tea gardens and local markets offer different ways to understand the region. A journey here can be more than a collection of photographs.

Travellers can learn from local food, crafts, music and festivals. They can stay in small homestays and speak with hosts about their surroundings. Such visits can support guides, drivers, artisans and family businesses. Yet tourism also brings pressure: litter, crowded roads and damage to fragile places.

Good tourism should therefore respect people and nature. Visitors can follow local rules, avoid plastic waste and pay fairly for services. Communities should have a voice in how tourism develops, rather than losing control of their homes to outsiders.

The North-East is not a single culture or a single landscape. Its beauty lies in its variety. A thoughtful visitor returns with memories and leaves the place worthy of another visit.

Four original conversation-comprehension drills

Use the board-style format: read each conversation, then answer the numbered questions in full sentences. Each set totals 10 marks; model answers follow. These passages and questions are original, not copied board questions.

Passage A: A school reading corner

Rina: Why are these books in the corridor, Kabir?
Kabir: Our class is setting up a reading corner. The library closes before some bus students can visit it.
Rina: Where will the books come from?
Kabir: Teachers have donated twenty books, and students may lend one each. We will write the owner's name inside every borrowed book.
Rina: Who will look after them?
Kabir: Two volunteers will take turns at lunch. They will record loans in a notebook and return the books on Fridays.
Rina: Can anyone use the corner?
Kabir: Yes, but the books must remain in good condition. Our aim is access, not a competition over who reads most.

  1. Why is the class setting up a reading corner? (2)
  2. Give two sources of books. (2)
  3. How will the volunteers manage loans? (2)
  4. What is Kabir's aim, and what must readers do? (2)
  5. Find words in the passage meaning (i) people who offer help without being required to; (ii) reach or opportunity to use something. (1 + 1)
Show answers

1. Some bus students cannot visit the library before it closes, so the class is creating another place to read. 2. Teachers donate books, and students lend books. 3. Two volunteers take turns at lunch, record loans in a notebook and return books on Fridays. 4. He wants access rather than a reading contest; readers must keep the books in good condition. 5. (i) volunteers; (ii) access.

Passage B: Choosing a course

Mother: You seem uncertain about your course application, Neel.
Neel: I enjoy biology, but my friends think I should study engineering.
Mother: Have you asked a teacher about your options?
Neel: Yes. Sen Ma'am suggested that I compare the subjects and talk to people who work in both fields.
Mother: That sounds sensible. Is the deadline close?
Neel: It is next Monday. I will visit the college website tonight and write down my questions.
Mother: What matters most to you?
Neel: I want a course that suits my interests and abilities, not merely one that sounds fashionable.
Mother: Then make a careful choice. We can discuss the costs together tomorrow.

  1. Which subject does Neel enjoy? (1)
  2. Why does he feel pulled towards engineering? (1)
  3. What two steps did Sen Ma'am suggest? (2)
  4. What will Neel do tonight and when is the deadline? (2)
  5. Explain Neel's view of a suitable course. (3)
  6. Find a word meaning “reasonable and practical.” (1)
Show answers

1. Biology. 2. His friends think he should study engineering. 3. Compare the subjects and speak to people working in both fields. 4. He will visit the college website and note his questions tonight; the deadline is next Monday. 5. It should match his interests and abilities; he does not want to choose merely because a course is fashionable. 6. sensible.

Passage C: A cleaner festival

Tara: Will the festival committee still give out plastic cups?
Imran: No. We have arranged steel cups that can be washed and reused.
Tara: Who will wash them during the event?
Imran: Volunteers will collect them at two return desks. A local group will wash them in clean water behind the hall.
Tara: What about the food stalls?
Imran: Stall owners have agreed to keep separate bins for food scraps and other waste. We will put clear signs above the bins.
Tara: Will all this be expensive?
Imran: We must pay a deposit for the cups, but we expect to get it back after returning them. Reducing waste is worth the effort.

  1. What will replace plastic cups, and why? (2)
  2. Where can visitors return used cups? (1)
  3. Who washes the cups and where? (2)
  4. What will stall owners do with waste? (2)
  5. Why does Imran expect to recover the deposit? (2)
  6. Find a word meaning “used again.” (1)
Show answers

1. Washable, reusable steel cups will replace disposable plastic cups. 2. At two return desks. 3. A local group will wash them in clean water behind the hall. 4. They will use separate bins for food scraps and other waste. 5. The committee expects the deposit back when it returns the cups. 6. reused.

Passage D: Sport and study

Coach: Why did you miss practice yesterday, Maya?
Maya: I had a science test this morning and needed extra revision. I should have informed you earlier.
Coach: Thank you for saying so. How can you manage both next week?
Maya: I can revise for an hour before dinner and attend practice on Tuesday and Thursday.
Coach: The match is on Saturday. Can you come for a short session on Friday too?
Maya: Yes, if it ends by five. My bus leaves at five fifteen.
Coach: Agreed. We will practise passing, not play a full match. Remember to rest on Friday night.
Maya: I will. I want to play well without neglecting my studies.

  1. Why did Maya miss practice, and what does she admit she should have done? (2)
  2. On which two days can she attend regular practice? (2)
  3. Why must Friday's session end by five? (1)
  4. What will the team practise on Friday instead of playing a full match? (1)
  5. How does Maya plan to balance sport and study? Give three details. (3)
  6. Find a word meaning “not giving enough attention to.” (1)
Show answers

1. She revised for a science test and admits she should have informed the coach earlier. 2. Tuesday and Thursday. 3. Her bus leaves at five fifteen. 4. Passing. 5. She will revise before dinner, attend two regular practices and join a short Friday session; she also intends to rest. 6. neglecting.
