Grammar: board-style mini-practice

The 2025 and 2026 Q12/Q13 both used question tags and error correction, rather than the broader grammar pattern of a different English subject. For a positive statement use a negative tag and vice versa; rarely, seldom, few, no usually make the clause negative. Match the auxiliary and subject, including aren't I?, shall we? after let's, and will you? for requests.

A. Add tags (try all eight; a board paper asks any five).

  1. The bus has left, ______?
  2. She doesn't play tennis, ______?
  3. Let's start now, ______?
  4. I am early, ______?
  5. Nobody called us, ______?
  6. He seldom misses class, ______?
  7. Please close the door, ______?
  8. Everyone enjoyed the play, ______?

Key: 1. hasn't it? 2. does she? 3. shall we? 4. aren't I? 5. did they? 6. does he? 7. will you? 8. didn't they? (Everyone takes singular verb in the statement but commonly they in the tag.)

B. Correct and rewrite each sentence (board style: any five of eight).

  1. One of my friend lives in Jorhat.
  2. She prefers tea than coffee.
  3. He is an university student.
  4. The teacher, along with her pupils, are ready.
  5. I have lived here since three years.
  6. Neither of the answers are correct.
  7. This is a useful informations.
  8. He is senior than me.

Key: 1. One of my friends lives in Jorhat. 2. She prefers tea to coffee. 3. He is a university student. 4. The teacher, along with her pupils, is ready. 5. I have lived here for three years. 6. Neither of the answers is correct. 7. This is useful information. 8. He is senior to me.

Final check: use the actual school paper's instructions for choices and marks; don't assume a particular essay, dialogue topic, or 160-word requirement will appear.
