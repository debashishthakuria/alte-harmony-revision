Grammar: board-style mini-practice

The 2025 and 2026 Q12/Q13 both used question tags and error correction, rather than the broader grammar pattern of a different English subject. For a positive statement use a negative tag and vice versa; rarely, seldom, few, no usually make the clause negative. Match the auxiliary and subject, including aren't I?, shall we? after let's, and will you? for requests.

A. Add tags (try all eight; a board paper asks any five).

  1. The bus has left, ______?
  2. She doesn't play tennis, ______?
  3. Let's start now, ______?
  4. I am early, ______?
  5. Nobody called us, ______?
  6. He seldom misses class, ______?
  7. Please close the door, ______?
  8. Everyone enjoyed the play, ______?
Show answers

1. hasn't it? 2. does she? 3. shall we? 4. aren't I? 5. did they? 6. does he? 7. will you? 8. didn't they? (Everyone takes singular verb in the statement but commonly they in the tag.)

Voice: active and passive

Active: the subject does the action: “The teacher praised Rina.” Passive: the object receives it: “Rina was praised by the teacher.” Keep the tense and meaning the same. Use a suitable form of be + the past participle (V3). Add “by” only when the doer matters. A sentence without an object usually cannot be changed into a normal passive sentence.

Quick forms: writes → is written; wrote → was written; is writing → is being written; has written → has been written; will write → will be written; can write → can be written.

Try these. Change the voice:

  1. Rina writes a letter.
  2. The boys cleaned the room.
  3. The chef is cooking dinner.
  4. They have repaired the road.
  5. The teacher will check the copies.
  6. Someone stole my bag.
Show answers
  1. A letter is written by Rina.
  2. The room was cleaned by the boys.
  3. Dinner is being cooked by the chef.
  4. The road has been repaired.
  5. The copies will be checked by the teacher.
  6. My bag was stolen.

Tense: choose the correct verb

Present simple: habits and facts (“She goes to school every day”). Present continuous: happening now (“She is studying now”). Present perfect: completed action with a link to now (“She has finished her work”). Past simple: finished past time (“She finished it yesterday”). Past continuous: action in progress in the past (“She was reading when I called”). Past perfect: earlier of two past actions (“She had left before I arrived”). Future simple: “She will return tomorrow.” Time words help, but check the whole sentence.

Try these. Use the correct form of the verb:

  1. She ___ (go) to school every day.
  2. Listen! The birds ___ (sing).
  3. We ___ (visit) our aunt yesterday.
  4. He ___ (finish) the work already.
  5. When I reached the station, the train ___ (leave).
  6. They ___ (play) football when it began to rain.
  7. I ___ (call) you tomorrow.
  8. She ___ (live) here since 2020.
Show answers
  1. goes
  2. are singing
  3. visited
  4. has finished
  5. had left
  6. were playing
  7. will call
  8. has lived
